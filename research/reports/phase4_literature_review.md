# Phase 4B — Targeted literature review (what is established, what TASC-v1 may and may not claim)

All DOIs below were checked against the Crossref API on 2026-11-03 (title, first author, venue, volume, pages, year). Items marked *(from Phase 3)* were verified in the earlier correction pass. Content statements are based on the cited abstracts/standard knowledge of the technique; I did not re-read full texts of SAGC/SAMSIE/DFFN in this pass, so claims about them are restricted to what their titles/abstracts state.

| Topic | Established technique | Reference | Consequence for TASC-v1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exposure-adaptive gamma | Gamma chosen from image statistics: AGC (Rahman 2016), AGCWD (Huang 2013, histogram-weighted CDF); mean-to-target gamma `ln τ / ln μ` is a textbook step. Key-value exposure mapping to a scene "key" and a high-percentile "white" point: Reinhard et al. 2002 (tone reproduction). | `rahman2016agc`, `huang2013agcwd`, `reinhard2002tonerep` | Adaptive exposure is **not novel**. TASC-v1 uses a highlight-anchored gamma (a percentile mapped to a white target) with a mean cap — a Reinhard-style key/white idea, justified here by the failure analysis (fixed τ forces dark scenes to 0.55). |
| Spatially adaptive gamma | SAGC for optical remote-sensing images (weak-structure preservation) and SAMSIE (spatially adaptive multi-scale enhancement) | `huang2018sagc`, `huang2022samsie` | Local gamma fields are **established**; neither is evaluated here (no official code was used; "2D-gamma" in the benchmark is a Lee-style re-implementation, *not* SAGC). |
| Retinex / illumination estimation | Reflectance × illumination; illumination = smooth component | `land1971retinex`, `jobson1997msr`, `he2013guided` | The surround-illumination estimate used for the local target is Retinex-style; no novelty claimed. |
| Shadow vs dark material | Intensity-only edges (illumination) vs chromaticity edges (reflectance): Finlayson 2006; invariant colour-space shadow detection/compensation in aerial images: Tsai 2006; review of detection/de-shadowing: Shahtahmassebi 2013; physically based de-shadowing of satellite/airborne imagery using a scene-derived illumination function: Richter & Müller 2005; cloud-shadow geometry: Zhu & Woodcock 2012 | `finlayson2006shadows`, `tsai2006shadow`, `shahtahmassebi2013shadow`, `richter2005deshadow`, `zhu2012fmask` | Shadow-vs-material cues are **established heuristics** and fail in known ways (blue-shifted shadows, dark water, burn scars). TASC-v1's confidence is a *heuristic score*, not a calibrated probability. |
| DEM topographic correction | Cosine/C-correction, SCS+C; Lambertian illumination `cos i` | `teillet1982ccorr`, `soenen2005scsc`, `horn1981hillshading` | DEM shadow modelling is **established**. |
| Spectral preservation | A single multiplicative factor per pixel preserves band ratios/NDVI exactly (algebraic); dark-object subtraction handles additive haze, not reflectance | `chavez1988dos` | Shared gain is a property, not a contribution; it does not recover reflectance. |
| Learning-based RS low-light | Zero-DCE (curve estimation); DFFN (spatial–frequency dual-domain network for low-light RS imagery) | `guo2020zerodce`, `yao2024dffn` | Compared (Zero-DCE) / discussed (DFFN, not evaluated; no weights obtained). TASC-v1 stays training-free. |

## What remains potentially new (to be tested, not assumed)
1. *Evidence-gated* local lift: the local gamma lift is multiplied by a shadow-confidence map built from cues that are individually established, with an explicit fall-back to *no lift* when evidence is absent (naturally dark scenes stay dark).
2. *DEM trust estimation*: DEM-derived shadow is used only to the extent it agrees with image darkness (alignment search + agreement score + sun-metadata ensemble), degrading gracefully to image-only for wrong/shifted DEMs.
Neither is claimed novel in itself; their value is an empirical question answered in the results. If they do not outperform strong simple baselines, the paper will say so.
