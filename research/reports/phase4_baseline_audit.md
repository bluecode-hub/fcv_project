## Diagnosis of the AID/iSAID failure (motivates Component 1)
`g0 = ln(0.55)/ln(mean Y)` forces *every* image to mean luminance 0.55 and the block stage pushes every 16 px block towards 0.55 again. Consequences: (i) clean AID images average well below 0.55, so the correction over-exposes (new-bright clipping is avoided only by the soft knee; `mae_bright` falls to 0.139 because highlights are compressed, but SSIM falls below the *input's*); (ii) block-wise equalisation flattens local contrast (SSIM 0.211 < identity 0.256); (iii) classification features computed on flattened, over-brightened images move out of the frozen classifier's distribution (−15…−24 pp). Removing only the global step recovers 4 dB, localising the main damage in the fixed-target exposure rule plus the unconditional local equalisation.

## Benchmark-structure caveat found during Phase 4 (relevant for interpretation)
The AID degradations are per-channel power laws (`a·x^γ`) which also change band ratios (chroma). A ratio-preserving (shared-gain) method cannot undo that change by construction, whereas per-band gamma can. Part of the per-band global-gamma advantage (PSNR 17.5 vs shared-gain global gamma 15.7) is therefore an artefact of the degradation model, not evidence that colour-distorting enhancement is preferable. Luminance-only diagnostics (PSNR-Y/SSIM-Y, added in Phase 4 as supplementary metrics) separate the two effects.
